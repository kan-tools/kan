# Feature: v0.7 milestone — the correctness release

## Summary

Three independent adversarial reviews of v0.6.0-beta.1 (issue #48's method,
dispatched as reviewers with no stake in the code) found roughly twenty
confirmed defects, about half of which destroy data. All three converged on
one boundary without being told to:

> kan is correct exactly where the design attention went — the fold and the
> cryptographic core — and absent everywhere data is keyed, framed, or
> rendered.

Track 1 put it as a table: of five lossy derivations in the codebase, four are
treated as authoritative and resolved last-writer-wins, and the only one
handled correctly is the one *designed* as a heuristic. Track 3 put it as
"the cryptographic core is sound; the framing layer around it is not." Track 2
put it as "every read surface reports its filtered view as if it were the
whole log."

`CLAUDE.md`'s non-negotiable invariant — *no operation destroys a subject* —
was enforced with real care in `fold/`, the one module that reads morphisms,
and never applied to `store/`, `transport/`, or `sign/`, the modules that
write bytes. That is a boundary that was never crossed, not twenty
independent slips, and this milestone is organized around the boundary rather
than around the defect list.

This release is therefore **not** a bugfix release and must not be described
as one. It is a correctness release, and it is the last release that may
break the on-disk format cheaply: the beta has one user, who is making that
call about their own data, and after this `docs/SPEC.md` §7.1's coexistence
contract has to carry every subsequent change. Every format break this
milestone intends is enumerated in REQ-1 and REQ-13 rather than discovered
during implementation, so that the break is taken once.

ADR-47 (`merge=union` withdrawn) already shipped ahead of this doc, on its
own PR, because it was the single finding where doing nothing was worse than
doing the obvious thing immediately.

## Requirements

### A. Local spine integrity — the log loses claims under ordinary use

- REQ-1: `ClaimContent` gains `recorded_at`, a microsecond-precision
  timestamp, signed and therefore inside the CID. Two clocks, deliberately:
  `rev` remains log-internal MST sequencing and never crosses the `Transport`
  boundary (`.design/v0.5-milestone.md` REQ-2 stands); `recorded_at` is the
  clock time of the recording *from the frame of the observer*, and is
  therefore content, not storage metadata. It is attested, not measured —
  clock skew between actors is honest data about differing frames rather than
  corruption to reconcile, and a lying author can attest any time, which runs
  the same trust machinery as every other claim. Cross-actor ordering by
  `recorded_at` is a projection over attested times, parameterized by a base
  the reader chooses, never a stored global truth (`telos/raw-data-and-projections`).
  Microseconds is chosen explicitly, not inherited: second-precision would
  silently reintroduce REQ-2's collision for any caller in a loop.
- REQ-2: Appending identical content twice produces two distinct claims. This
  is the direct consequence of REQ-1 and is stated separately because it is
  the defect being fixed: today `Log::append` keys the MST by the content CID
  (`src/store/log.rs`), so identical content overwrites, the CLI reports
  success both times, and one claim survives. Three further defects collapse
  with it — an existing record's `rev` is silently rewritten so history
  reorders, a re-appended claim can be pushed past its own retraction leaving
  a live `Retraction` coexisting with its live target and toggling
  indefinitely, and it fires on ordinary agent traffic (`kan mark task open`
  twice yields one claim) because `artifacts` pins `HEAD`.
- REQ-3: Concurrent appends from separate processes do not lose claims.
  Today `.kan/log/` has no lock: each process opens the CAR into an in-memory
  MST, appends, and last-writer-wins overwrites `HEAD`, so five parallel
  `kan observe` calls return five distinct CIDs and five exit-0 successes
  while two claims survive. Reproduces at N=2. The losers' blocks reach the
  CAR but are unreachable from the winning root. This is the product's stated
  deployment — one human, one-or-more local agents — and `day` shells out to
  the same binary, so it is reachable without anything unusual happening.
  ADR-15's claim that reopening from disk every call "avoids any question of
  concurrent-mutation safety" is exactly inverted and is corrected by this
  milestone: reopening per call is *what makes* each process start from a
  stale root.
- REQ-4: A crash or torn write during append never leaves the log
  unreadable. Today `persist_new_blocks` calls `flush()` rather than
  `sync_all` and then writes `HEAD` non-atomically, which permits `HEAD` to
  become durable before the blocks it points at; and `Log::open_or_create`
  has no fallback at all — no CAR-header root, no scan, no previous-commit
  walk — so a torn CAR tail or a partial `HEAD` write bricks reads *and*
  writes while every claim sits intact on disk.
- REQ-5: A repository that is moved or renamed retains its identity. Today
  the keychain entry holding the signing key is keyed by the canonicalized
  filesystem path of `.kan/identity` (`src/sign.rs`), so moving a checkout
  misses the lookup, silently generates a new keypair and DID, and
  `TrustBase::Solo` then drops every prior claim: `kan status` prints "no
  subjects yet" at exit 0, and retracting your own pre-rename claims fails
  with "you can't retract another author's claim." A `did:key` is meant to be
  self-certifying; its retrievability must not hang on a mutable environment
  string.
- REQ-6: `KAN_AGENT` and `derive_agent_key` are **removed**, not repaired.
  Rationale in Resolved Questions.

### B. GitTree — the sharing layer corrupts honest input

- REQ-7: A claim's narrative text round-trips byte-exactly. Today the writer
  emits text verbatim and the reader `.trim()`s it, so 8 of 22 tested cases
  break — trailing newline, leading/trailing space, tab indent,
  whitespace-only, CRLF, NBSP (`str::trim` eats U+00A0), and runs of blank
  lines — each producing "the record has been altered since it was signed"
  against an honest claim. It is unrecoverable, because the CID is frozen in
  the append-only log, and silent, because `publish` exits 0. A trailing
  newline is the common case for any multi-line text arriving over MCP.
- REQ-8: No narrative content can alter the record framing. Today
  `RECORD_SEPARATOR` is a bare substring split with no escaping in the
  writer, so the literal separator in prose splits one claim into three
  records: a tampering accusation, a malformed record, and a phantom.
- REQ-9: Every field a human reads in the frontmatter is authenticated
  against the claim it describes, or is not written at all. Today `author`,
  `subject`, `kind` and `cites` are documented "derived, ignored on read" and
  never cross-checked, so each can be set to an arbitrary lie that verifies
  clean: a reviewer reading a PR diff sees a `Decision` by a victim's DID
  about a different subject citing a fabricated provenance edge, while kan
  reads an `Observation` by the real author citing nothing. The format's
  entire purpose is human review in a PR, and human review is the one thing
  the verification chain does not currently protect. `cites` being forgeable
  is a direct hit on `CLAUDE.md`'s "provenance is sacred: never fabricate or
  drop `cites` edges."
- REQ-10: Deleting a record from a published file is detectable. Today
  removing a whole record leaves the remainder verifying cleanly — there is
  no manifest, count, or chain linking a subject's records — so anyone with
  repo write access can delete a shared claim and nothing in kan will ever
  say so, and an out-of-date clone republishing does it accidentally via
  `write_subject`'s whole-file rewrite.
- REQ-11: `KnownBody` gains `#[serde(deny_unknown_fields)]`. ADR-44's fix
  landed on `ClaimContent` only, so `ClaimBody`'s custom `Deserialize` never
  reaches its `Unknown`-preservation branch for a *known* kind carrying a
  future field: serde accepts it, drops the field, and the transport then
  reports "altered since it was signed" — verbatim the behaviour ADR-44
  measured and claims to have eliminated. Latent until the next body-shape
  change, and ADR-46 explicitly weighed a `reason` field on `Relation`.
- REQ-12: `kan publish` writes only the claims a reader would call live, and
  reports a count that agrees with `kan show`. Today it binds a variable
  named `live` from `iter_all` filtered *only* by subject — no retraction
  filter, no trust filter — so a subject showing 2 live claims published 5,
  including a retracted observation and another agent's claim, rendered
  identically to live ones.
- REQ-13: The subject-to-filename mapping is injective, and the filename is
  authenticated against the records inside. Today every non-alphanumeric
  character collapses to `_`, so `telos/legible-process` and
  `telos_legible-process` share one file and the second publish destroys the
  first — the sanitizer's own doc comment names that exact example, and `day`
  is the real consumer of that naming scheme. On APFS, case-insensitivity
  collides `Bug42` and `bug42` on top. The filename is also never compared to
  the frontmatter, so a file's apparent subject is decorative.
- REQ-14: The record format carries a version, per the original
  `.design/git-tree-transport.md` REQ-4/AC-5, which was never implemented in
  `src/`.
- REQ-15: `kan publish --all` exists, per the original REQ-9, which was never
  implemented.
- REQ-16: The concurrent-merge story is stated and tested against a real
  `git merge`, replacing ADR-43's withdrawn `merge=union` guidance (ADR-47).
  The design must account for records being emitted in CID-lexicographic
  order rather than append order, since that is what falsified ADR-43's
  "a conflict at the tail is informative" premise.

### C. Read surfaces — none of the above is detectable from inside kan

- REQ-17: `show` exposes what a claim actually carries: `cites`, `artifacts`,
  author, and `recorded_at`. Today `cites` and `artifacts` appear zero times
  in every read path, and no read surface shows a timestamp or author at all.
- REQ-18: A read verb accepts a CID, so a `Retraction`'s `supersedes`
  pointer resolves. Today `kan show <cid>` treats it as a subject name and
  returns "no claims", leaving `docs/SPEC.md` §8's "a view CAN show 'X
  retracted Y'" half-built — you see *that*, never *what*.
- REQ-19: `context` reports what it omitted, and ranks globally by claim
  value rather than by subject-name sort order. Today it round-robins one
  claim per class in lexical order and prints only what it kept: at budget
  150 over 14 claims it emitted five observations and dropped the only
  `Status{Blocked}` and its `Blocker` narrative with zero signal, because
  `task-1` sorts before `task-3`. Budget 0 renders identically to an empty
  log. `kind_value`'s scoring is applied only *within* a class today, which
  inverts the module doc's own stated purpose.
- REQ-20: `context` and `show` never present superseded and current status
  claims as peers. Today both emit `Status: Resolved` and `Status: Blocked`
  unlabeled, in score order rather than chronological, with no supersession
  marker — while `kan status` correctly reports `Settled(Resolved)`.
  `context` is the surface designed to be pasted into an agent's window.
- REQ-21: Relations are visible from both endpoints, and `fold::relations` is
  reachable. Today `kan relate a blocks b` then `kan show b` omits the edge,
  and `edges`/`in_tension_with`/`tension_witnesses` — the entire symmetric-
  projection module `docs/SPEC.md` §4.5.1 was amended for, shipped in the two
  commits currently unreleased on `main` — is called by nothing outside its
  own test module.
- REQ-22: Both surfaces describe themselves accurately. `relate`'s CLI help
  and MCP tool description omit `InTensionWith`, which both accept; MCP
  JSON-Schema `description` fields leak Rust implementation doc comments
  verbatim into agent context (`mark`'s `value` parameter documents
  `schemars`-versus-`clap` trait choices); and `issues` labels subjects
  `[Local("x")]` while `show` accepts only the bare name, so `issues` output
  is not copy-pasteable into `show`.
- REQ-23: `kan status`/`kan issues` do not advertise classifications that
  cannot occur. `classify` reduces to one live position per `AuthorId` and
  the fold under `Solo` has already discarded every author but one, so
  `Contested`/`Confirmed` are unreachable from the CLI and MCP while the MCP
  instructions tell the agent `status` returns them. Reachability is v0.8's
  trust surface; honesty about it is this release's.
- REQ-24: Issue #62 is re-characterized before it is fixed. The reported
  behaviour ("`issues` lists subjects whose every claim has been retracted")
  did not reproduce under block-then-retract-everything —
  `has_status_claim` goes false and the class is correctly skipped. The
  trigger is narrower than reported and must be identified rather than
  guessed at.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] AC-1: Appending identical `ClaimContent` twice yields two distinct
      CIDs and `kan show` reports two claims. (REQ-1, REQ-2)
- [ ] AC-2: A claim retracted, then re-recorded with identical text, does not
      resurrect: the retraction stays honored and no live `Retraction`
      coexists with a live target. (REQ-2)
- [ ] AC-3: `kan mark <s> open` run three times yields three claims, and
      `kan same a b` run twice yields two witnesses. (REQ-2)
- [ ] AC-4: N parallel `kan observe` processes against one log produce N
      claims, all reachable from the final root, for N ≥ 5 — run as a real
      multi-process test, not simulated in-process. (REQ-3)
- [ ] AC-5: Killing the process mid-append, and separately truncating the CAR
      tail and corrupting `HEAD`, each leave a log that still opens and reads;
      no state leaves both reads and writes failing while claims are intact
      on disk. (REQ-4)
- [ ] AC-6: A repo directory renamed after claims are recorded still shows
      every claim, under the same DID, with `kan retract` still accepting the
      author's own pre-rename claims. (REQ-5)
- [ ] AC-7: `KAN_AGENT` no longer appears anywhere in `src/`, and kan's own
      `.mcp.json` no longer sets it. Claims written over MCP are visible to
      CLI reads in the same repo. (REQ-6)
- [ ] AC-8: Every case in track 3's round-trip matrix — trailing newline,
      leading/trailing space, tab indent, whitespace-only, CRLF, NBSP,
      repeated blank lines — publishes and parses back to a matching CID.
      (REQ-7)
- [ ] AC-9: Narrative text containing the literal record separator publishes
      and parses back as exactly one claim with a matching CID. (REQ-8)
- [ ] AC-10: Editing `author`, `subject`, `kind`, or `cites` in a published
      record's frontmatter to any valid-but-false value is reported as an
      error naming the mismatched field — not accepted silently. (REQ-9)
- [ ] AC-11: Deleting a whole record from a published file is reported when
      the file is read back. (REQ-10)
- [ ] AC-12: A record whose body is a *known* kind carrying an unknown field
      is preserved and re-encodes byte-identically, rather than being
      reported as altered since signed. (REQ-11)
- [ ] AC-13: `kan publish` on a subject with retracted and foreign-author
      claims writes only the live trusted ones, and its reported count equals
      `kan show`'s. (REQ-12)
- [ ] AC-14: `telos/x` and `telos_x` publish to different files, both
      readable; and a file whose name disagrees with its records' subject is
      reported. On a case-insensitive filesystem, `Bug42` and `bug42` do not
      collide. (REQ-13)
- [ ] AC-15: A published file carries a format version, and a record from a
      declared-future version produces an actionable error naming the version
      rather than a CID mismatch. (REQ-14)
- [ ] AC-16: `kan publish --all` brings every published subject's file up to
      date. (REQ-15)
- [ ] AC-17: Two divergent clones' published files, merged with a real
      `git merge` under whatever guidance this milestone ships, either
      preserve both sides' claims or raise a visible conflict — never exit 0
      having destroyed a claim. (REQ-16)
- [ ] AC-18: `kan show` displays `cites`, `artifacts`, author and
      `recorded_at` for a claim carrying them. (REQ-17)
- [ ] AC-19: `kan show <cid>` of a retracted claim displays that claim's
      content. (REQ-18)
- [ ] AC-20: At a budget that cannot fit everything, `kan context` names how
      many claims and which subjects it omitted, and a `Status{Blocked}`
      outranks an `Observation` regardless of the two subjects' names.
      (REQ-19)
- [ ] AC-21: After `kan block s` then `kan resolve s`, neither `context` nor
      `show` presents both statuses as peers. (REQ-20)
- [ ] AC-22: `kan relate a blocks b` is visible from `kan show b`, and
      `fold::relations` has at least one caller outside its test module.
      (REQ-21)
- [ ] AC-23: `relate`'s help on both surfaces lists `InTensionWith`; no MCP
      schema description contains Rust implementation vocabulary

- [ ] AC-24: No CLI help or MCP description promises `Contested` or

## Architecture

Work is organized by the boundary the reviews found, not by defect list.
Files named here are the ones the reviews identified as causal.

**`src/claim.rs`** — `ClaimContent` gains `recorded_at` (REQ-1); `KnownBody`
gains `deny_unknown_fields` (REQ-11). Both are format breaks and both land in
the same PR so the break is taken once.

**`src/store/log.rs`** — `append` stops being the whole story. The MST key
stays the content CID (it is the claim's identity per `docs/SPEC.md` §1, and
`cites` edges are CIDs, so two claims sharing one CID would make citation
ambiguous — that is why the discriminator must live in `ClaimContent` and
cannot be fixed by re-keying storage). Concurrency (REQ-3) and durability
(REQ-4) are this file's: a lock around the read-modify-write of `HEAD`, an
`fsync` ordering that makes blocks durable before the root that points at
them, an atomic `HEAD` replacement, and an `open_or_create` fallback path.

**`src/sign.rs`** — the keychain account key stops being derived from the
canonicalized path (REQ-5).

**`src/workspace.rs`** — `derive_agent_key` and the `KAN_AGENT` read are
deleted (REQ-6); `my_author` returns the human-direct `AuthorId`. `.mcp.json`
at the repo root drops the `env` block.

**`src/transport/git_tree.rs`** — the largest single surface. Round-trip
fidelity (REQ-7), separator framing (REQ-8), header authentication (REQ-9),
deletion detection (REQ-10), filename injectivity and authentication
(REQ-13), format version (REQ-14), and the merge story (REQ-16) all live
here. `GITATTRIBUTES_LINE` is already empty (ADR-47).

**`src/actions.rs`** — `publish`'s filtering and count (REQ-12), `--all`
(REQ-15), and most of section C's read surfaces: `show`'s fields (REQ-17),
CID lookup (REQ-18), supersession display (REQ-20), relation visibility
(REQ-21), `issues`' subject labels (REQ-22), #62 (REQ-24).

**`src/context.rs`** — omission reporting and global ranking (REQ-19),
supersession (REQ-20).

**`src/mcp.rs`** — descriptions (REQ-22, REQ-23).

**`src/fold/`** — deliberately almost untouched. Every review found its keys
are the real values and its invariant handling is sound; the one change is
wiring `fold::relations` to a caller (REQ-21). If this milestone finds itself
rewriting the fold, that is a signal the diagnosis was wrong.

## Resolved Questions

- **Time goes inside the CID.** Settled by elimination rather than
  preference: the MST key is the content CID, so identical content
  overwrites; re-keying the MST by `cid+rev` would keep both records but
  leave two distinct claims sharing one CID, and since `docs/SPEC.md` §1
  makes the content CID the claim's identity with no explicit id field and
  `cites` edges are CIDs, any citation of that CID becomes ambiguous. That is
  provenance failing at the data model, so the discriminator must live in
  `ClaimContent`. The same argument eliminates signing time outside the CID.
- **Two clocks, not one.** `rev` is a sequencing clock for MST storage
  internals; `recorded_at` is the observer's own frame-relative record of
  when they claimed something, and is therefore content interpretation.
  Promoting `rev` into `ClaimContent` was rejected: it would collapse two
  semantically different things because they share a shape, weld storage
  ordering to claim semantics permanently and in the CID, and reverse
  `.design/v0.5-milestone.md` REQ-2's considered position on grounds of
  convenience while that position's reasoning still holds.
- **`recorded_at` is the right fix for the duplicate-claim defect**, though
  the coupling was questioned. A nonce would fix distinctness without
  committing time to every CID, but the sharing layer needs portable
  ordering regardless, so a nonce means shipping both; and a random nonce
  would make genuine idempotency impossible, not merely inconvenient.
- **`KAN_AGENT` is deleted, not repaired.** It is documented in its own
  source as a placeholder that is "not a real keypair and nothing verifies it
  against anything"; kan's own `.mcp.json` sets it, so the shipped
  configuration makes the agent surface and the human surface read disjoint
  views of one log by default; and v0.9's per-agent identity work replaces it
  wholesale. Repairing something already scheduled for deletion, in the
  release whose theme is that provisional patches cause data loss, would be
  the wrong lesson. Removing it narrows `AuthorId` usage rather than widening
  it, which is the direction v0.9 wants anyway.
- **"Fully fix GitTree" does not require shipping a reader**, with a stated
  test. `from_record`/`read_all` have no caller in the binary, which is why
  every detection result in track 3 required linking the crate directly. v0.7
  makes the format correct, verifiable and safe, and proves it through the
  library plus tests; v0.8 makes the CLI consume it. The test that would move
  the reader into v0.7: if the acceptance criteria for REQ-9, REQ-10 or
  REQ-13 cannot be demonstrated without a shipped reader, the reader moves
  and v0.8 shrinks accordingly.
- **`Contested` is not made reachable here.** Reachability needs the
  `PeerContested` trust surface, which is v0.8's milestone. This release's
  obligation is to stop promising what it cannot deliver (REQ-23).
- **The format break is accepted, for a reason that expires.** The beta has
  exactly one user, who is making this call about their own data. This is the
  last release where that argument is available. `docs/SPEC.md` §7.1's
  coexistence contract carries everything afterwards, and this milestone is
  its first real exercise — which is also why REQ-11 matters, since the
  contract's own worst case is currently unfixed at one of its two levels.

## Out of Scope

- **Wiring `Transport` through `Workspace`** so a published tree is read by
  the CLI — v0.8's milestone, unchanged by this release except that it
  shrinks if the reader moves here (see Resolved Questions).
- **The `PeerContested` trust surface** — v0.8. This release only stops
  advertising what it cannot deliver.
- **Real per-agent cryptographic identity (issue #30)** — v0.9. `KAN_AGENT`'s
  deletion here is removal of a placeholder, not a replacement for it.
- **`HostedRelay`, `AtProto`, lexicons** — v0.10 and beyond,
  `.design/sync-layer-architecture-and-staging.md`.
- **Incremental fold (#25), vector index (#15), keychain UX friction (#69)** —
  unrelated to this milestone's boundary. #69 is noted as a v0.9 prerequisite
  in ADR-35 and stays there.
- **Generalizing `M(A,B)` to `M_kind(A,B)` (#72)** — a fold-layer enrichment
  question, and the fold is the one layer these reviews found sound.
- **ADRs should be hard claims (#75)** — a standing question about where
  kan's own architecture record lives, not a correctness defect.
